Design and Fabrication of an Active and Instrumented Arm Prosthesis

Development of a functional prosthetic arm integrating mechanical design, sensors, actuators, and embedded electronics.

I’m proud to present the Design and Fabrication of an Active and Instrumented Arm Prosthesis — a multidisciplinary engineering project aiming to combine mechanical design, electronics, and biomedical innovation.

The objective of this project was to design and build a functional prototype of a robotic arm prosthesis capable of mimicking the natural motion of the human arm. The system integrates multiple subassemblies — motorized and instrumented fingers, a functional hand with five actuated digits, and a forearm designed to host the embedded control electronics.

The project followed a structured engineering workflow. We first established a comprehensive specification sheet defining parameters such as maximum load capacity, finger and wrist motion range, thermal limits, and ergonomic constraints. Leveraging the InMoov open-source design as a base, we modeled the prosthesis under CATIA V5, integrating actuators and sensors directly into the digital design.

The prototype components were 3D printed and assembled after validation of the digital mock-up. Several experimental tests were conducted to verify motion precision, control feedback, and the overall mechanical robustness of the system.

This project not only strengthened our skills in mechanical design and additive manufacturing but also deepened our understanding of mechatronic integration — bridging the gap between mechanical engineering and embedded systems.
