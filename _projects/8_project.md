<div class="lead mt-4">
  I’m proud to present the <strong>Design and Fabrication of an Active and Instrumented Arm Prosthesis</strong> — a multidisciplinary engineering project aiming to combine mechanical design, electronics, and biomedical innovation.
</div>

<p>
  The objective of this project was to <strong>design and build a functional prototype</strong> of a robotic arm prosthesis capable of mimicking the natural motion of the human arm. 
  The system integrates multiple <strong>subassemblies</strong> — motorized and instrumented fingers, a functional hand with five actuated digits, and a forearm designed to host the <strong>embedded control electronics</strong>.
</p>

<p>
  The project followed a structured engineering workflow. We first established a <strong>comprehensive specification sheet</strong> defining parameters such as maximum load capacity, finger and wrist motion range, thermal limits, and ergonomic constraints.  
  Leveraging the <strong>InMoov open-source design</strong> as a base, we modeled the prosthesis under <strong>CATIA V5</strong>, integrating actuators and sensors directly into the digital design.
</p>

<p>
  The prototype components were <strong>3D printed</strong> and assembled after validation of the digital mock-up. Several <strong>experimental tests</strong> were conducted to verify motion precision, control feedback, and the overall mechanical robustness of the system.
</p>

<p>
  This project not only strengthened our skills in <strong>mechanical design and additive manufacturing</strong> but also deepened our understanding of <strong>mechatronic integration</strong> — bridging the gap between mechanical engineering and embedded systems.
</p>
